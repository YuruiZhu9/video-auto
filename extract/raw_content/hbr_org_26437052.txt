When Using AI Leads to “Brain Fry”
==================================

A new study finds that certain patterns of AI use are driving cognitive fatigue, while others can help reduce burnout. by [Julie Bedard](/search?term=Julie Bedard), [Matthew Kropp](/search?term=Matthew Kropp), [Megan Hsu](/search?term=Megan Hsu), [Olivia T. Karaman](/search?term=Olivia T. Karaman), [Jason Hawes](/search?term=Jason Hawes) and [Gabriella Rosen Kellerman](/search?term=Gabriella Rosen Kellerman)

March 5, 2026![]()HBR Staff/Runstudio/Getty Images

On New Year’s Day, programmer Steve Yegge launched [Gas Town](https://steve-yegge.medium.com/welcome-to-gas-town-4f25ee16dd04), an open-source platform that lets users orchestrate swarms of Claude Code agents simultaneously, assembling software at blistering speed. The results were impressive, but also dizzying. “[T]here’s really too much going on for you to reasonably comprehend,” [wrote](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-01-15-a-day-in-gas-town/) one early user. “I had a palpable sense of stress watching it. Gas Town was moving too fast for me.”

She advises leaders across industries on AI strategy and people transformation.![]()[Matthew Kropp](/search?term=Matthew%20Kropp&amp;search_type=search-all)  is a Managing Director and Senior Partner at Boston Consulting Group, CTO of BCG X, and a BCG Henderson Institute Fellow. His work and research focus on implementing AI in the enterprise and shaping the relationship between agents and humans at work.![]()[Megan Hsu](/search?term=Megan%20Hsu&amp;search_type=search-all)  is a Project Leader at Boston Consulting Group and an Ambassador at the BCG Henderson Institute. Her research focuses on how AI and agents are reshaping the nature of work and value creation.![]()[Olivia T. Karaman](/search?term=Olivia%20T.%20Karaman&amp;search_type=search-all)  is a PhD candidate at the University of California, Riverside, where she studies the expectations, waiting, uncertainty, and emotional well-being. She is also a research assistant at Boston Consulting Group.![]()[Jason Hawes](/search?term=Jason%20Hawes&amp;search_type=search-all)  is a PhD candidate at the University of California, Riverside, where he studies impatience as an emotion, and the roles of mindfulness and flow in well-being. He is also a research assistant at Boston Consulting Group.![]()[Gabriella Rosen Kellerman](/search?term=Gabriella%20Rosen%20Kellerman&amp;search_type=search-all)  is an Expert Partner and Director at Boston Consulting Group and the co-author, with Professor Martin Seligman, of Tomorrowmind. A psychiatrist by training, Kellerman focused research on organizational performance in uncertainty and leadership in the era of generative AI.

## Abstract
Unsupervised anomalous sound detection (ASD) aims to identify unknown abnormal sounds in machines using normal sounds. Most unsupervised ASD methods extract log-Mel [spectrogram](/topics/engineering/spectrogram) features of machine sounds and directly employ them all for [anomaly detection](/topics/engineering/anomaly-detection), whereas the sound characteristics of different machine types are not considered. It has been proved that distinguishing the sound characteristics across different machine types is important for anomaly detection. To address the issue, a similarity-based automatic classification method is proposed for common machine sounds, based on the temporal characteristics of sounds. Furthermore, a similarity-based feature selection strategy is presented for different sound categories. Finally, the selected features are employed by a classic \*β\*-VAE for anomaly detection. [Comparative experiments](/topics/engineering/comparative-experiment) were made with several competing ASD methods on the datasets of the DCASE 2020 Task2 and DCASE 2022 Task2. The results demonstrate the performance superiority of our method over relevant competing unsupervised models.
## Introduction
Anomalous sound detection (ASD) has attracted much attention over decades. It has widespread applications [1], [2], including predictive maintenance of equipment [3], machine condition monitoring [4], medical diagnostics [5] and audio surveillance [6], to name a few.
In industrial scenarios, the ASD tasks aim to distinguish between normal and abnormal sounds emitted by a target machine or device [7], [8], [9]. Since 2020, the IEEE Audio and Acoustic Signal Processing (AASP) Technical Committee has included the industrial ASD as a challenging task in the Detection and Classification of Acoustic Scenes and Events (DCASE) competition, encouraging global researchers to explore and advance this field. Based on the definition of anomalies, ASD tasks can be roughly divided into two types. For one type, the anomalous sounds are clearly defined [10], [11], and the model is normally trained in a supervised behavior. For the other type, the anomalous sounds are not clearly defined or unknown. In the second case, the anomalous sounds usually refer to those with large deviations from the normal or reference pattern, and the outlier-detection techniques are usually employed [4]. In this paper, we focus on this type of ASD.
In real-world factories, anomalous sounds occur rarely and have high variability [1], and it is non-trivial to collect anomalous data. Therefore, the unsupervised ASD methods that do not require anomalous sounds for model training have been widely developed. In early anomaly detection research, various statistical models such as GMM [12], SVM [13], and HMM [14] were used to model normal sounds and calculate anomaly scores for anomaly detection. These methods usually relied on mathematical assumptions about data distributions or sequences. While effective for simpler sound patterns, they often struggled to capture the complex and high-dimensional nature of sound signals, resulting in suboptimal performance.
In the past decade, deep learning-based unsupervised ASD has achieved significant progress. Conventionally, Autoencoders (AE) [15] and Variational Autoencoders (VAE) [16] have been widely applied in unsupervised learning tasks [1], [17], where they compressed and reduced the dimensionality of input features and then reconstructed the input features from the reduced representations. Their powerful feature representation capabilities and simple, easy-to-train network structures lay the cornerstone for an encoder-based ASD model. Duman et al. [18] proposed an end-to-end unsupervised ASD system based on convolutional autoencoders. Girl et al. [19] introduced a group-masked autoencoder for density estimation of audio time series. Despite capturing frame-level statistics, the approach relied heavily on predefined frame-level structures, limiting its adaptability to diverse machine sound types. Suefusa et al. [20] proposed the IDNN method to enhance reconstruction for non-stationary sounds by masking and reconstructing central frames. However, it focused on isolated frames neglected the broader temporal context, leading to potential inaccuracies in detecting anomalies distributed over time. Instead of employing the reconstruction error in model training, Koizumi et al. [4] designed an objective function based on the Neyman-Pearson lemma, which trains the encoder-based model to maximize the true positive rate (DPR) under low false positive rate (FPR) conditions. Overall, these unsupervised ASD methods learned the feature pattern of normal sounds and detected anomaly with the learned pattern, achieving excellent performance on specific datasets. However, for complex detection scenarios [21], such as domain shifts [22], [23] (i.e., there are distribution discrepancies between the source and target domains), their detection performances may significantly decline. To address this issue, many researchers are devoted to improving the model's stability and adaptivity. For instance, Yan et al. [22] proposed the Transformer and Dynamic Graph Convolution (Unsuper-TDGCN) for anomaly detection under domain shifts. It employed Transformer to fully learn the local and global dependencies of time-frequency domain features, and designed a Dynamic Graph Convolution Network (DyGCN) to fully capture content-aware features of sound signals from different machine IDs and dynamically capture feature relationships from machine IDs under domain shifts by using content-aware features, improving the model's stability and adaptivity under shifts. Chen et al. [23] proposed a multi-scale dual-decoder autoencoder (MS-D2AE) model, which leverages a Multi-scale Feature Fusion Module (MTSFFM) to learn normal data features. By incorporating a dual-decoder structure and residual connections, the model enhanced the reconstruction accuracy and anomaly detection capabilities. Moreover, a feature domain mixing method was designed to improve its robustness against domain shifts.
In addition to unsupervised methods, self-supervised learning-based ASD has attracted much attention [24]. Normally, the self-supervised ASD methods introduce additional information such as machine IDs [25], [26] to train an effective classifier for anomaly detection, enabling the models to effectively focus on distinguishing the features for abnormal sounds. Ritwik et al. [27] utilized MobileNetV2 to create a classification framework for anomaly detection, which aims to determine the machine ID of audio samples. Liu et al. [28] proposed a spectral-temporal information fusion-based method, which combines the temporal information extracted by a CNN network with the spectral information from log-Mel spectrograms for anomaly detection. Similarly, Kota et al. [29] introduced an auxiliary model to distinguish targets within the same machine type from other machine IDs, aiming for precise likelihood estimation for each machine ID. Moreover, contrastive learning has been an promising alternative in self-supervised ASD. Hadi et al. [30] first introduced contrastive learning into the ASD framework. By employing data augmentation and contrastive loss, it learned the representations from unlabeled data. Guan et al. [31] proposed the CLP-SLF method, which combines the machine IDs with a self-supervised ID classifier and optimizes the audio representation of each machine ID through contrastive learning. This approach not only utilized labeled samples but also trained the model to recognize mixed proportions of different IDs through random combinations. In the research conducted by Yang et al. [32], a dual attention structure is proposed based on contrastive learning, which aimed to learn a permutation-invariant representation for anomaly detection.
In all, most self-supervised ASD methods obtained superior performances over unsupervised ones, by leveraging additional information (i.e., machine IDs or attribution information) during training. Nevertheless, it is unrealistic to expect that such information will always be available in real-world scenarios. Moreover, it has been proven that distinguishing sound characteristics across different machine types is important for anomaly detection. Practically, temporal characteristics of most machine sounds may be roughly grouped into two categories [20], [33]: stationary sounds and non-stationary sounds. For stationary sounds, the related target sound event is dispersed among almost the whole time interval, where most time periods are dominated by the target sound accompanied by noise or interference. For non-stationary sounds, the related target sound event is dispersed among a very few time periods, where most time periods are dominated by noise or interferences. Thus, employing the sound features from the whole time interval may be not optimal for anomaly detection. It is rational to select the feature of partial time intervals that contain the main information of target sounds for anomaly detection, according to the temporal characteristics of different sounds.
In this paper, an unsupervised ASD method is proposed, based on similarity-driven automatic feature selection. First, the log-Mel spectrogram features are extracted from the raw audio. Then, considering the temporal characteristics of most machine sounds, a similarity-based automatic classification strategy is developed. Furthermore, a similarity-based feature selection strategy is presented. Finally, the selected features are fed into a \*β\*-VAE model for anomaly detection. Reconstruction error is used as an anomaly score to determine whether the sound is anomalous. In comparison with our previous work in [34], where the sound stationarity classification of common machine types is assumed as known prior information, an automatic classification strategy is developed herein and such prior information is assumed as unknown. The contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows:
1) Based on the temporal characteristics of common machine sounds, a similarity-based automatic classification method is developed, without knowledge of the stationarity classification prior of common machine types.
2) A similarity-based selection strategy is presented, which aims to select the time frames associated with the target sounds from the log-Mel spectrogram. This may alleviate the influences of noises and other redundant factors from the original sound signals.
3) By combining the automatic classification and feature selection, an unsupervised ASD method SCFS-VAE is developed. This method achieves expected detection performance by selectively learning the audio features after classifying the sound signals.
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 provides a detailed overview of the proposed SCFS-VAE method. Section 3 presents the related experiments and analysis to validate our method. Section 4 summarizes some conclusions of this study.

\* ### [MSANet: Multi-Stage attention network for anomalous sound detection in machine condition monitoring](/science/article/pii/S1051200425006487)
2026, Digital Signal Processing A Review Journal
Show abstract
Anomalous Sound Detection (ASD) system identifies sound waves from sensors to detect the anomaly of industrial machines. However, recent methods have failed to sufficiently focus on partial details and long-term dependence information in acoustic features, resulting in poor performance on certain machine types. To address this challenge, we propose a novel ASD model based on the multi-stage attention network (MSANet). The spectral-temporal concatenated spectrogram of the audio samples is used as the MSANet input, and serially modeled by the network. The fusion spectrogram attention network (FSAN) enhances inter-spectrogram correlation via directional pooling and attention weighting. Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) is used in the local attention network to focus on the channel and spatial information in acoustic vectors, hence improving capacity of ASD system for modeling local information. In global attention network, the gated recurrent unit (GRU) is applied to improve the feed-forward layer of transformer, enhancing the model to capture global correlation feature and contextual information. Extensive experiments are conducted out on the DCASE 2020 Challenge Task 2 dataset to evaluate the proposed model. Experimental results demonstrate that MSANet achieves an average AUC of 94.89 % and an average pAUC of 89.11 %, both of which surpass the performance of previously methods.

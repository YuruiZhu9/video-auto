Artificial intelligence is more a part of our lives than ever before. While some might call it hype and compare it to NFTs or 3D TVs, generative AI is causing a sea change in nearly every part of the technology industry. OpenAI’s ChatGPT is still the best-known AI chatbot around, but with Google pushing Gemini, Microsoft building Copilot, and Apple adding its Intelligence to Siri, AI is probably going to be in the spotlight for a very long time. At The Verge, we’re exploring what might be possible with AI — and a lot of the bad stuff AI does, too.

How the New York Times vets images before publishing them.
Unsurprisingly, considering President Trump’s [tendency](https://www.theverge.com/news/787042/trump-posts-then-pulls-bizarre-ai-video-promoting-medbed-conspiracy) to [post](https://www.theverge.com/2024/8/19/24223589/trump-ai-generated-swift-harris-social-media) AI [slop](https://www.theverge.com/news/802321/organizers-say-over-7-million-showed-up-to-no-kings-protests), the \_New York Times\_ wasn’t exactly keen on publishing his supposed photo of Venezuelan President [Nicolás Maduro](https://www.theverge.com/news/853728/the-u-s-attacked-venezuela-and-arrested-president-nicolas-maduro) on board the USS Iwo Jima without question. Ultimately, the paper couldn’t verify the image’s authenticity but decided to [publish](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/01/04/insider/how-the-times-assessed-maduro-photos.html) it in the context of his Truth Social post. The \_Times’\_ Director of Photography Meaghan Looram detailed their skepticism:
> It looked odd: The photo was cropped to an unusual, vertical shape, suggesting that much of the content of the original image had been excluded, and the quality was low. One Times photo editor noted that it looked like a photo of a printout, or a photo of a screen. When run through the same A.I. detector, the image posted by Mr. Trump also left some uncertainty.

Why hang a painting in your home, when you can tell an AI to put slop on your wall?

It’s meant to mimic your typical wall art but, because nothing is sacred, instead of showing an actual painting or a photo by a human, it displays AI “art” generated by your voice prompts. The Smart Canvas is available for [preorder](https://fraimic.com/collections/shop) in two sizes, standard for $399 and large for $999, though deliveries aren’t expected until June.
